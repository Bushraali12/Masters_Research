Official TCIA partition

Protocol A

The train/test division published with the dataset. 1,318 crops from 691 patients for training; 378 crops from 201 patients held out. No patient appears on both sides. This is the harder and more comparable evaluation — the held-out set is 38.9% malignant against 48.3% in training.

04

Two-stream classifier Contribution 1

Two DenseNet-121 encoders run in parallel: one on the tight crop at 512 px, one on a 1.75× wider crop at 384 px. Features are pooled twice — once weighted by the predicted mask, once uniformly — and concatenated.

One stream studies the lesion itself; the other studies the tissue around it. The mask makes the lesion count more without discarding its surroundings.

mask weighting w = 1 + 2·mask · 4,096-d feature · focal loss

05

Fusion with radiological assessment Contribution 2

The network's probability is combined with three fields the radiologist already recorded — the BI-RADS category, breast density, and lesion subtlety — by a logistic model fitted on the training partition alone and then frozen.

The image and the radiologist disagree in different ways. Where the radiologist is already certain, the model defers; where the assessment is ambiguous, the image decides. Combining them beats either alone.

inputs: log-odds of CNN probability, BI-RADS one-hot, density, subtlety · threshold fitted on training data under a 0.70 sensitivity floor

Five-fold cross-validation

Protocol B

The same pipeline, evaluated the way most of the literature does it. All 892 patients are divided into five groups; each group is held out in turn while the other four train. Every patient is tested exactly once, and no patient ever appears in both sides of a fold.

01

Patient-grouped folds

Folds are formed over patients, not images, so both projections and every lesion belonging to one woman always move together.

If the same tumour appeared in training and testing, the score would be meaningless. Grouping by patient makes that impossible.

Segmentation and two-stream classification Contribution 1

Identical architecture to Protocol A — the attention U-Net produces the mask, the two DenseNet streams read the tight and wide crops, and mask-weighted pooling combines them.

Nothing about the method changes between the two protocols. Only which patients are used for training changes.

03

Fusion, refitted inside every fold Contribution 2

The fusion model and its decision threshold are refitted from scratch on each fold's four training groups and applied to the held-out group. They never see the data they are scored on.

The gain is larger here than on the official partition, because training and testing have the same proportion of cancers — so the threshold lands where it was designed to.

Reading the two protocols together. Both report the same method on the same data; they differ only in which patients are used for training. Discrimination exceeds 0.90 in all four cells, and the two protocols agree to within 0.03 AUC — evidence that neither estimate is an artefact of how the data was divided.

What the fusion result means. Rows marked + fusion combine the image with the radiologist's recorded assessment, so they are not image-only results and are reported separately from the two-stream rows throughout. At lesion level on the official partition the fusion raises sensitivity from 0.816 to 0.897 — nine missed cancers instead of sixteen — at the cost of six additional false positives.
