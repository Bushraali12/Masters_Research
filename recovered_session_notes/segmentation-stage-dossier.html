01What goes into the network

Not the full mammogram. Lesion-centred crops cut from it. This is the single most important thing to be clear about, because it changes what the Dice number means.

A full mammogram is roughly 4,000 × 5,000 pixels and the lesion occupies a fraction of one percent of it. Segmenting that directly is a detection problem — find the needle, then outline it. This pipeline solves the second half only: given a region that contains a lesion, draw its boundary. That is the standard formulation for CBIS-DDSM and it is what makes the numbers comparable to the literature in §6.

How each crop is made

step 1
Locate the annotationThe radiologist's mask is read from the full-resolution mammogram and its bounding box computed.
step 2
Choose a window sizeSide length is set so the lesion occupies 23% of the crop area, with a floor of 1.15 × the lesion's longest dimension so the whole lesion always fits. Fixing coverage rather than pixel size means the network sees every lesion at a comparable scale.
step 3
Frame it on tissueAn Otsu breast mask is computed and the largest connected component kept; the window is constrained to breast tissue, which removes the half-black crops that naive edge cropping produces.
step 4
Slide, never inventIf the window runs past the image edge it is slid inward. 153 of 1,696 windows needed this. No pixel is ever synthesised. 58 crops still have the lesion touching an edge — those are lesions that genuinely run off the mammogram, which no crop can recover.
step 5
Resample and equaliseCrop written at 512 × 512, downsampled to 256 × 256 for segmentation, contrast-equalised with CLAHE (clip 2.0, tile 8 × 8).

Achieved geometry. Lesion coverage: median 23.0%, range 12.1–23.1%. Every crop contains only real mammographic tissue. This uniformity is deliberate and it is part of the contribution — see §7.

02The architecture, layer by layer

The network is a deeply supervised attention U-Net with an atrous spatial pyramid pooling bottleneck. Four components, each doing a specific job.

Encoder — four stages, 32 → 64 → 128 → 256 channels

Each stage is two 3×3 convolutions with batch normalisation and ReLU, followed by 2× max-pooling. Early stages see fine detail over a small area (edges, grain); later stages see coarse structure over a large area (is this a round dense object?). Resolution falls, semantic content rises.

ASPP bottleneck — multi-scale context at 16 × 16

Five parallel branches look at the same features with different sampling spacings: a 1×1 convolution, three 3×3 convolutions at dilation 6, 12 and 18, and one global-average-pool branch. They are concatenated and projected back to 512 channels. Why it matters here: your lesions span 12% to 23% of the crop, so the network must judge scale without being told it.

Attention gates — on every skip connection

A plain U-Net concatenates the whole encoder feature map into the decoder, irrelevant tissue included. An attention gate takes the decoder's coarse semantic signal g and the encoder's detailed skip x and produces a per-pixel weight:

α = σ( ψ( ReLU( Wg·g + Wx·x ) ) )   →   x̂ = α ⊙ x

Every weight matrix is a 1×1 convolution; ψ maps to a single channel and the sigmoid puts α in [0, 1]. The network decides which fine details are worth keeping.

Deep supervision — four heads, weights 1.0 / 0.5 / 0.3 / 0.2

The loss is applied at four decoder depths, not just the last one, with the ground truth nearest-neighbour downsampled to match. This forces the middle of the decoder to produce something meaningful instead of relying on the final layer to repair everything. Only the finest head is used at inference — the other three exist purely to shape training.

The loss

L = Σd wd [ 0.3·CE + 0.7·(1 − Tverskyα=0.7, β=0.3) ]

Tversky generalises Dice by weighting false positives and false negatives separately. Setting α > β penalises missing lesion tissue more heavily than over-segmenting it — the right asymmetry when the object may be a cancer.

The property that makes the whole pipeline honest. Each fold's segmentation model writes predicted masks only for patients it never trained on. Those predictions — never the radiologist's outlines — are what the classifier consumes downstream. Patient disjointness is asserted in code before every fold begins.

Three crop generations exist. The Dice differences between them are differences in the data, not the model — the architecture was identical in all three runs.

Why 0.9200 fell to 0.9062, and why that is correct. In v2, 153 crops contained a band of Gaussian noise where the window ran past the image. Noise has no tissue texture, so the network classified it as background for free — and every one of those pixels counted toward Dice. The 0.9200 was inflated by an artefact I introduced. In v4 that region contains real breast parenchyma, which can resemble a lesion and must actually be discriminated. 0.9062 is measured on harder, honest data. It is the number to report.

05The ablation table, explained

Three output heads were trained on identical folds, identical crops, identical seeds and identical schedules. The final layer is the only thing that differs. That is what makes this a valid ablation.

HeadDicestdΔ vs standardWhat it is
ds standard0.92000.0030— 1×1 conv → 2 channels
duel0.92030.0028+0.0003 V + A − mean(A), the Dueling-DQN decomposition
duel20.92010.0029+0.0001 repaired: coarse region stream supervised on a dilated mask

Five patient-grouped folds each, on v2 crops. Because all three saw identical data, this comparison remains valid regardless of the later crop fix.

Read this correctly: the heads are indistinguishable. The largest difference is +0.0003, while the fold-to-fold standard deviation is 0.0030 — ten times larger. And duel is worse than ds on fold 3. This is noise, not a gain, and claiming +0.0003 as an improvement would not survive a paired test.

But you know why — and that is the valuable part

For a two-class softmax output, the dueling head is provably degenerate:

zc = V + Ac − ½(A0+A1)   ⟹   z0 − z1 = A0 − A1

The value stream cancels. Softmax and cross-entropy depend only on the logit difference, so V has no effect on the output and receives exactly zero gradient — you measured 4.3 × 10⁻⁹, which is float32 noise. The dueling head in this setting is mathematically identical to a plain 2-channel convolution.

You then built duel2, which repairs the degeneracy by giving the two streams genuinely different supervision — and showed the problem is structural, not an implementation detail.

07What is novel, and what is not

This is the section to read twice before meeting your supervisor. Being precise here protects you; over-claiming does not.

Not novel — do not claim these

U-Net (Ronneberger 2015) · attention gates (Oktay 2018) · ASPP (Chen, DeepLab) · deep supervision (Lee 2015) · Tversky loss (Salehi 2017) · CLAHE.

Assembling them well is competent engineering, and the result is strong. But if you present the combination as an architectural novelty, a reviewer will cite all five original papers back at you. Say instead: "a well-established segmentation backbone, chosen so that the contribution is isolated elsewhere."

Novelty 1 — architecture

A dueling value/advantage head for dense segmentation, with a proof of its degeneracy and a repaired variant.

Nobody has transferred the Wang et al. (2016) decomposition to pixel-level prediction. You did, you proved analytically that it cannot work for binary segmentation, you confirmed the proof numerically (zero gradient), you confirmed it empirically across five folds (+0.0003 against 0.0030 fold noise), and you built duel2 to show the failure is structural rather than an implementation error.

A neutral result you can prove is worth more than a small gain you cannot explain.

Novelty 2 — method

A leakage-controlled, geometry-controlled segmentation protocol that makes the downstream classifier honest.

  • Predicted masks generated only for patients each fold never trained on — the radiologist's outline never reaches the classifier, so the pipeline is end-to-end deployable.
  • Coverage-normalised crop geometry (fixed 23%) so every lesion is seen at a comparable scale — and, measurably, this removed the size dependence (Spearman +0.039).
  • Zero synthetic pixels, verified by geometric audit.
  • One partition shared by both stages, patient disjointness asserted in code.

If your supervisor wants a novelty with a positive number attached, it lives in Stage 2, not Stage 1. The mask-guided dual-pooling classification head — where the predicted mask weights features (w = 1 + 2m) rather than gating them, pooled twice and concatenated — is worth +0.0705 AUC over an architecturally identical mask-blind control, and the accompanying ablation shows that gating instead costs −0.11 AUC. That is a genuine architectural contribution with a measured effect and a mechanistic explanation: the diagnostic signal lives at the lesion margin, which a gate discards.

Say this if challenged:

I use the complete CBIS-DDSM mass cohort — all 1,696 annotated regions from 1,005 lesions in 892 patients, with no BI-RADS category or pathology class excluded — plus external validation on INbreast. The 163 GB figure refers to 16-bit DICOM storage of both cohorts; I work from the 8-bit JPEG conversion of the identical cases, and I quantify that limitation directly: a measured median of 80 distinct grey levels inside lesion regions, against 16-bit originals.

You are not hiding the limitation — you measured it. That is a stronger position than most people who will ask the question.
